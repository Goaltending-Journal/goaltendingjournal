At a glance

What the research supports, kept apart from what it doesn't

The research supports

  • Practice performance can mislead in both directions.
  • Retention and transfer tests can give opposite verdicts.
  • Freeze rate is far steadier than save % at practice volumes.

It doesn't show

  • A validated coach-rated goalie measure. None exists.
  • That freeze rate predicts goals saved. The link is weak.
  • How many reps make a reliable retention check.

01

Looking sharp isn't learning

Performance is how it looks today. Learning is what lasts and transfers. Performance during practice is a temporary state that can mislead both ways: learning can happen without visible gains, and gains can appear without lasting learning.[1]

A goalie who looks great at the end of Tuesday's drill has shown performance. Whether it stuck shows up next week, cold, and in a different setting.

02

Retention and transfer

You need both checks, because they can disagree. In a small boxing study, the group given prescriptive instruction did better on retention, while the constraints-led group improved from retention to transfer.[2]

  • Retention block. Re-run a standard block a week or more later, at the start of a session, with no feedback.
  • Transfer check. Look for the same read in a changed setting: a different shooter pattern, a scrimmage or game clips.
  • Keep the test game-like. A closed lateral-push sequence rewards drill-specific practice and misses adaptability. Many soccer trials had the same flaw.[10]
03

Measure process, not save %

Save percentage needs about 3,000 shots to reach half signal, and a goalie may face a few hundred practice shots in a week.[3] Practice save % is mostly noise.

Process outcomes are steadier. Frozen-shot percentage repeated year to year at r = 0.58, against 0.26 for adjusted rebound rate.[4] But freeze rate explained only about 16% of goals saved in one Swiss league analysis.[5] So freeze rate is a good sign of whether a habit is changing, which is the learning question, and a weak sign of overall quality. 04

Check yourself too

Coaching behaviour can be measured. A research coding system tracks 23 coach behaviours such as instruction, feedback, questioning and management.[7] A simple tally from one filmed session tells you whether you mostly ask or mostly tell, and how long your goalie is moving versus listening. If module 4's feedback plan said "trial and error", the tally shows whether you stuck to it.

Check your understanding

Your goalie stops 18 of 20 lateral passes at the end of Tuesday's drill, up from 12 of 20 at the start. What does that tell you?

05

Close the loop

Reading isn't enough. In a survey of 320 coaches, 73% of responses showed new ideas being applied without critical thought, and reflection made up about 2% of knowledge sources.[8] The one study that filmed coaches changing their practice paired online content with weekly reflective conversations. It had three coaches and no control group, so treat it as a hint.[9]

  1. Try one change from this path.
  2. Film it.
  3. Reflect: what did I change, what did the goalie do, what will I adjust?
  4. Check it cold next week.

Then take it to a brief: Glove Positioning Analysis has a ready-made "Try it this week".
